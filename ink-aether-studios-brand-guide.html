Section 01 — Studio Identity

What Ink & Aether Studios Is

Not an author. Not a platform. A standard.

Ink & Aether Studios publishes fiction where worlds feel inevitable — like they were always real and you only just found them. Every title, every author, every story under this imprint shares one promise: someone made this on purpose.

The Studio Model — What It Is and Isn't

Ink & Aether Studios operates as a boutique literary press — not a co-author brand, not a "presents" label, and not a secondary pen name. Think Pixar, not Disney Presents. Think Criterion Collection, not Adapted from a Book By. The studio name does not lend credibility to a project; it certifies it.

When a reader sees the Ink & Aether colophon, they are not being told who wrote the book. They are being told something about what kind of book it is — the care behind it, the intention in it, the craft standard it was held to.

The studio currently houses three pen names: J.P. White, Jade Black, and Indigo Winter. Each is a distinct imprint with its own brand identity. The studio is the structure they all live inside — and the structure is invisible until readers learn to recognize it.

The Studio Promise

There is one thing every Ink & Aether title must earn before it carries the colophon:

Does this world feel like it was already real before we arrived? If yes: this is an Ink & Aether Studios book.

This question applies regardless of genre. A cozy gothic mystery in the Blue Ridge Mountains must feel as geographically inevitable as a solarpunk Neo-Egyptian fantasy. A romantic suspense must feel as emotionally real as a hard-science thriller. The promise is not about tone or subject — it is about the specific gravity of fully-built worlds.

Studio Personality — What Every Studio Asset Conveys
  • Quiet authority. The studio does not announce itself. It is noticed.
  • Craft-first confidence. The work is good enough to let silence do the marketing.
  • Archival permanence. Built like something meant to still be here in twenty years.
  • Human scale. One founder. Three pen names. Six books a year at most. That is a feature, not a limitation.
  • Zero performance. The studio does not perform busyness, productivity, or hustle. It publishes books and leaves it at that.
Section 02 — Logo & Mark

The Studio Mark

An inkwell. Rising aether. A wax seal. The tools of the trade, held in the moment before the first word.

The mark above is a working compositional reference. The production logo should be commissioned as a vector asset in Canva or via a logo designer using this brief as the specification. Logo Mark — Component Description

The Wax Seal A small circular wax seal to the right of the inkwell, bearing the I&A monogram — suggesting authentication, closure, and the mark of a press The imprimatur. The studio's certification that what follows meets the standard.

Logo Usage Rules
  • Always use the mark with clear space equal to the height of the "I" in Studios on all four sides
  • Use Primary Dark version as the default — it is the most versatile
  • Scale the full lockup (mark + wordmark + "Literary Press") together — never separate elements
  • Minimum size: 80px wide for digital; 0.75 inch for print
  • Never place on a busy photographic background without a contained dark card or overlay behind it
  • Never recolor the mark outside the four approved versions
  • Never stretch, rotate, or alter proportions
  • Never use the mark in a context that implies humor, lightness, or parody
  • Never use just the inkwell icon without the wordmark in public-facing materials until brand recognition is established

Color Use Rules

The Single Metallic Rule

Gilded Ink (#C9A96E) is the only metallic in the studio palette. It appears exactly once per design in a primary accent role. When it appears as a rule line, it does not also appear as a headline color. When it appears in the logo, it does not appear again as a background tint. One appearance. Maximum authority.

Temperature Rule

The studio palette reads as neutral-cool — not warm like J.P. White, not plum-deep like Jade Black. Never warm up the studio palette with golden backgrounds or saturated tones. The gold is metallic and measured, not warm and inviting. There is a difference.

Section 05 — Voice & Tone

How the Studio Speaks

The studio voice is distinct from all three pen names. It does not tell stories. It introduces the people who do.

Voice Characteristics
QualityWhat It Sounds LikeWhat It Does Not Sound Like
Authoritative but not distant"Ink & Aether Studios publishes three writers. All of them are worth your time.""We're so excited to share..."
Proud without performing"This book took three years. It shows.""After YEARS of hard work we are THRILLED to..."
Sparse and unhurriedShort sentences. Confident pauses. Lets the work speak first.Lists of adjectives. Hype language. Superlatives.
Institutional with warmthThe warmth is in the precision — the care taken — not in exclamation points.Casual, chatty, or confessional
Singular perspectiveOne founder. One standard. The studio's credibility lives in its specificity.Royal "we" used insincerely; anonymous corporate voice
Voice Examples — Studio vs. Imprint

Launch Announcement

Studio voice (Ink & Aether): "The Ember Mill Haunting publishes Tuesday. J.P. White's second book. Read it in one sitting or take your time — it rewards both."

Imprint voice (J.P. White): "I've been carrying this story for two years. Blackvale is waiting. See you Tuesday — Sylvia and Miles are ready."

Annual Recap

Studio voice: "Year one: four books published. Zero debt accumulated. All three pen names active. This is what sustainable looks like from the inside."

About the Press

Studio voice: "Ink & Aether Studios is an independent literary press. We publish fiction where worlds feel inevitable. We are one founder, three pen names, and a standard we do not negotiate."

The Studio Does Not
  • Perform vulnerability or exhaustion publicly — that is the founder's personal voice, not the studio's
  • Engage in publishing discourse, controversy, or platform debates
  • Compare itself to other indie publishers or traditional publishing
  • Apologize for its prices, its pace, its decisions, or its choices
  • Use hype language: "game-changing," "unmissable," "stunning," "you've never seen anything like this"
  • Ask for validation from readers, reviewers, or the market
Section 06 — Imprint Architecture

The Studio & Its Imprints

Three distinct brands. One publishing standard. The studio is the house. The imprints are the rooms.

Imprint One J.P. White

Southern gothic mystery and atmospheric fiction. Blue Ridge fog, bayou heat, coastal secrets. Literary warmth with rot underneath. The genre house of the studio.

Imprint Two Jade Black

Romance, gothic fantasy, and romantic suspense. Sensual, dramatic, emotionally devastating. The heart house of the studio.

Imprint Three Indigo Winter

Speculative fiction — sci-fi, fantasy, action-driven world-building. Cosmic, precise, defiant. The horizon house of the studio.

Hierarchy of Appearance — When the Studio Shows

The studio name does not compete with the imprint name on any individual book. The relationship follows the Criterion/Pixar model:

What the Studio Brand Is NOT
  • A fourth pen name or author identity — the studio has no books of its own
  • A "presents" label that borrows the author's reputation — it certifies independently
  • Louder than any individual imprint on any individual book — the studio whispers; the pen name speaks
  • Tied to any single genre, tone, or audience — it spans all three imprints deliberately
Section 07 — Studio Presence

Where the Studio Lives

Infrastructure foundation now. Public presence built deliberately toward later.

Phase One — Infrastructure (Now Through 2027)

The studio brand does not need a public-facing presence before the catalog exists. The Pixar model only works because Pixar had a catalog. Build the foundation now; activate the public presence when there are enough titles to introduce the pattern.

Active Now
Publishing Infrastructure

Copyright pages, spine credits, "Published by" KDP lines, ISBN registration, legal entity if applicable. The studio exists on the books before it exists in public.

Active Now
Studio-Voice Launch Graphics

A small number of launch assets in the studio's voice — distinct from pen-name launch assets. These introduce the press as a certifying entity around major launch milestones.

Active Now
Author Bios — Studio Credit Line

All three pen name bios include the phrase: "An Ink & Aether Studios author." This plants the studio name in every reader's peripheral vision from the first book forward.

Active Now
Internal Brand Documentation

This document. Brand Kits in Canva. The AETHER Framework. These exist to keep the studio consistent across years and pen names even before a reader ever sees the mark.

Phase Two — Public Presence (2027–2028 Target)

When the catalog reaches six or more titles across multiple pen names, the studio's public presence can begin. The Pixar pattern: readers who loved one pen name's book discover another pen name's book through the shared studio mark. The cross-promotion becomes organic.

Phase Two
Studio Social Account

A single Ink & Aether Studios account (Instagram primary) that introduces the studio, its three authors, and its standard. Content: catalog spotlights, studio milestones, cross-imprint discovery posts.

Phase Two
Studio Website

A minimal site — not a blog, not a newsletter hub. A landing page: the three pen names, the studio's statement, a catalog. The digital equivalent of a publisher's colophon page.

Phase Two
Media Kit

A press-ready document: studio overview, all three pen names introduced, catalog list, sales data (if shareable), and contact information. For libraries, retailers, and press inquiries.

Phase Two
Cross-Imprint Discovery Assets

Back matter language: "If you loved this book, Ink & Aether Studios publishes two other authors whose work you may find inevitable." With covers. With links.

What the Studio Palette Avoids
  • Warm, amber-toned overlays — those belong to J.P. White
  • Plum, rose, or dramatic romantic elements — those belong to Jade Black
  • Deep indigo, neon, or circuit-grid textures — those belong to Indigo Winter
  • Watercolor, floral, or hand-painted elements — no imprint uses these at studio level
  • Bright backgrounds, white fields, or high-key photography — the studio lives in the dark
  • Generic "publisher" stock imagery (stacks of books, quills on paper) — the mark is the visual, not stock photos
